title:          "Formative Cities: Early-Life Exposure to Urban Density and the Making of Universalist Outlooks"
selected:       true
abstract: >-
  Does place influence social and political outlooks? There is increasing evidence of a correlation, but little consensus on causation. Residents of large cities are, on average, more likely to hold values linked to universalism, rather than particularism. Yet, it has proven hard to isolate compositional effects from contextual effects. We provide novel causal evidence showing that exposure to urban density influences individual outlooks beyond compositional effects, but this influence is only driven by the environments where individuals spend their preadult life. We do this by using household panel data from both Switzerland and the UK, which allows us to combine 'Movers' and Two-Stage-Least-Squares research designs. We show with two different approaches that exposure to urban density before age 20 causally increases support for cosmopolitanism,  gender equality and LGBTQ+ rights, highlighting the lasting influence of place early in life.
